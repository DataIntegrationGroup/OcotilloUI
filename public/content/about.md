## What is Ocotillo?

Ocotillo is where Bureau staff go to access, view, and edit time-series data associated with monitoring points across New Mexico: wells, springs, and other sites where field observations are collected over time.

It is developed and maintained by the Data Services team at the [New Mexico Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources](https://geoinfo.nmt.edu), in support of the statewide [New Mexico Water Data Initiative](https://newmexicowaterdata.org).

## Who is it for right now?

The current version is designed for Bureau staff, particularly the [Aquifer Mapping Program (AMP)](https://geoinfo.nmt.edu/resources/water/amp/home.html). The immediate goal is straightforward: get data in, view it, and edit it.

Public access and broader data sharing are on the roadmap but are not the current focus.

## What data is available?

The portal currently provides access to:

- **Wells:** Water well inventory records including construction details, depth, and monitoring status
- **Locations:** Geographic points linked to monitoring sites and field programs
- **Contacts:** People and organizations associated with monitoring sites
- **Water chemistry:** Sample results and lab observations from field events
- **Geothermal and oil & gas:** Additional datasets will be brought in as programs onboard

## Why was it built?

The New Mexico [Water Data Act](https://geoinfo.nmt.edu/resources/water/data-act/home.html) passed in 2019 with a goal of improving water data availability and access across state agencies. The Bureau is the convening agency.

One of the things the process revealed was that data sharing problems exist within the Bureau itself. Programs that need the same data can't always find it or access it.

> "Ocotillo is really an attempt to improve our data sharing within the Bureau of Geology." Rachel Hobbs, Water Data Program Manager
